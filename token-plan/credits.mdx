Credits are the unit of consumption on Qwen Cloud. Every API call you make draws from your monthly credit pool, and the number of credits deducted depends on which model you use and how many tokens your request contains. Understanding how credits work helps you choose the right plan and make the most of your monthly allotment.

## What is a credit?

A credit represents a unit of AI compute capacity. When you send a request to any Qwen Cloud model, the platform counts the tokens in your input (the prompt) and your output (the response), converts that usage into credits, and deducts the total from your balance.

<Note>
  Token-to-credit conversion rates vary by model. More capable or resource-intensive models generally cost more credits per token than lighter models.
</Note>

## How credits are consumed

Credit consumption follows this pattern for every request:

<Steps>
  <Step title="You send a request">
    Your tool or application sends a prompt to a Qwen Cloud model endpoint using your API key.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The model processes your prompt">
    Qwen Cloud routes the request to the selected model, which generates a response.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Credits are deducted">
    The platform counts your input tokens plus output tokens, applies the per-model rate, and deducts the total credits from your monthly balance.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Your balance updates">
    Your remaining credit balance decreases immediately. You can check it at any time in the [usage dashboard](/account/usage).
  </Step>
</Steps>

Factors that affect how many credits a single request uses:

- **Model choice** — Different models have different credit rates per token.
- **Prompt length** — Longer system prompts and conversation histories consume more input tokens.
- **Response length** — Longer generated responses consume more output tokens.
- **Modality** — Image generation and speech requests are priced differently from text requests.

## Monthly credit allotments

Your credit balance resets at the start of each billing cycle. The allotment depends on your plan:

| Plan | Monthly credits | Price |
|---|---|---|
| Standard | 25,000 | $30/month |
| Pro | 100,000 | $100/month |
| Max | 250,000 | $200/month |

Credits do **not** roll over to the next month. Unused credits expire when your billing cycle resets.

<Tip>
  If you consistently hit your credit limit before the month ends, upgrading to the next tier is almost always cheaper than the overage cost on a pay-as-you-go basis—you already save at least 40% by subscribing.
</Tip>

## Viewing your credit usage

You can monitor your remaining credits and per-model consumption from the Qwen Cloud dashboard:

1. Log in to your Qwen Cloud account.
2. Open the **Usage** section from the account menu.
3. View your current balance, daily usage breakdown, and per-model consumption.

For a detailed breakdown of API call history, cost per request, and trend charts, visit the [account usage page](/account/usage).

## What happens when you run out of credits

When your monthly credit balance reaches zero, Qwen Cloud stops processing new API requests for your account until your balance resets.

<Warning>
  When your credits are exhausted, API calls return an error indicating insufficient credits. Your tools and integrations will not generate responses until credits are restored.
</Warning>

Here is what you can do if you run out of credits:

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Wait for your monthly reset">
    Your credits reset automatically at the start of your next billing cycle. No action is required. The exact reset time matches the date you first subscribed.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Upgrade to a higher-tier plan">
    If you regularly exhaust your credits, upgrading to Pro or Max gives you a larger monthly pool. Visit the [pricing page](/token-plan/pricing) to compare tiers and subscribe to a new plan.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Optimize your usage">
    Review your usage patterns in the [usage dashboard](/account/usage). Shortening prompts, reducing conversation history length, and choosing lighter models for simpler tasks can significantly reduce credit consumption without affecting output quality.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
